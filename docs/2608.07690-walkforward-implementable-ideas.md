# Paper Digest: arxiv-2608.07690 — Implementable Ideas for Walk-Forward ML Alpha Engine

**Paper:** "On a Simple Relationship Between Order Imbalance, Skew and Width in Over-The-Counter Trading" — Peter Cotton (arXiv 2608.07690v1 [q-fin.TR], 7 Aug 2026)

**Core thesis:** For an OTC/RFQ dealer whose customer flow is imbalanced (probability q of a seller arrival) and whose best competing quote is exponentially distributed around fair value (market width w = 1/hazard), an elementary identity — q·e^(−hS) + (1−q)·e^(hS) = 2√(q(1−q))·cosh(h(S−δ)) — compresses the *imbalanced* market-making problem exactly onto the *balanced* one. The imbalance is absorbed by three closed-form adjustments with **zero free parameters beyond observable width w**: (1) skew translated by δ = (w/2)·log(q/(1−q)), (2) non-discretionary width Δ = w+ε widened by γ = w·log(1/(2√(q(1−q)))), (3) effective cost of carry multiplied by M(q) = 1/(2√(q(1−q))) ≥ 1. Consequences: a flat-book dealer should still skew (Cor. 1: δ ≈ 2w(q−½) for mild imbalance); skew responds at **first order** in imbalance while width responds only at **second order** (Cor. 2 — why practitioners skew before they widen); one-sided flow is indistinguishable from a multiplied carry tax (Cor. 3). Constant-width-linear-skew (Avellaneda-Stoikov) survives as exactly optimal in one corner: balanced flow + cosh/quadratic holding cost, with imbalance supplying its "missing column" (the intercept δ and widening γ).

**Honest framing up front:** Despite the "market making + RL" tag, this is NOT an RL paper and NOT a prediction paper. It is an analytic microstructure result about *quote setting* in sealed-bid dealer markets. RL appears once (§5: the (δ,γ,M(q)) correction lets a learner pool episodes across flow regimes, shrinking required sample size). It makes **no empirical claims**. There are no fills, no quote archives, and no width observation in our daily LightGBM walk-forward pipeline — so most formulas are unusable literally. The transferable content is **one feature family** (flow imbalance), **two design principles** (first-order reposition vs second-order de-risk; pool-with-corrections instead of regime-splits), and **one diagnostic pattern** (decision-level scorecard separating signal-reading from management).

## Concrete implementable ideas, ranked by relevance

### A. Signed flow-imbalance features (the one direct signal export)
The whole paper turns on a single observable: signed arrival imbalance q (seller vs buyer share of flow), which shifts the conditional fair price by δ ∝ log-odds of direction. Microstructure cousin: Cartea et al.'s order-flow imbalance as predictive signal (cited §1.2).
- **Port:** add rolling *signed* imbalance features to the walk-forward feature set — coarse OHLCV proxies now (up-day vs down-day volume sums over 5/10/20d, close-location value (close−low)/(high−low), signed multi-horizon volume deltas); tick-grade Lee-Ready/tick-rule OFI only if trade-and-quote data ever lands.
- Keep the feature **continuous and signed** (like log-odds(q−½)), not bucketed into "regimes" — the paper's point is that imbalance is a smooth state variable, and continuous encoding lets one pooled GBM learn the interaction (see C).
- **Effort:** S (coarse proxies) / M (tick-grade). **Verdict: ADOPT-NOW** for coarse proxies — cheap, orthogonal to price-momentum features, and the existing expanding-window/annual-refit/5d-embargo harness evaluates it automatically; drop if null. Tick-grade OFI: TEST-LATER pending data.

### B. First-order reposition, second-order de-risk (Corollary 2 as a sizing rule)
Skew (where you position) moves linearly in imbalance; width (how much total risk you run) moves quadratically.
- **Port:** if/when a sizing layer exists, tilt *target exposure* linearly in the imbalance signal, but only cut *gross exposure / widen risk limits* when |imbalance| is severe (quadratic region). Avoids the common error of treating a mild one-sided-flow signal as a reason to halve position size.
- **Effort:** S (as a rule once sizing exists). **Verdict: TEST-LATER** — depends on a sizing/gating layer landing first (cf. 2608.19389 digest idea B/C).

### C. Imbalance-conditioned effective carry in cost-aware gating (Corollary 3)
One-sided flow ≡ multiplied cost of carry, M(q) = 1/(2√(q(1−q))), unbounded as q→0/1. Holding a position against persistent one-sided flow is taxed harder than symmetric-flow intuition suggests — dealers who ignore this "systematically overstate the cost… and encourage overly defensive quoting" cuts both ways.
- **Port:** in an act-or-hold gate (`trade only if expected_edge > roundtrip_cost`), make the *hold* side state-dependent: effective carry of keeping yesterday's position scales with current flow imbalance against that position. Concretely: raise the indifference threshold for continuing to hold when trailing imbalance opposes the position.
- **Effort:** M. **Verdict: TEST-LATER** — gated trading isn't built yet; this is the natural second variable after the static-cost version.

### D. Pool-with-correction instead of regime-splitting (§5 "statistical power of learned policies")
The symmetry lets a learner trained at one imbalance level operate at all others via the (δ,γ,M(q)) correction — "a learner can pool episodes across flow regimes instead of learning each regime separately," shrinking sample requirements. For us: never train separate models per vol/flow regime; encode the regime as corrected continuous features and let the annual-refit pooled model own the interaction. Directly supports the expanding-window design (more pooled rows per fit).
- **Effort:** S (design discipline, zero code). **Verdict: ADOPT-NOW** as a standing rule for feature engineering — costs nothing, prevents a foreseeable per-regime-model refactor.

### E. Decision-level scorecard (§5 last use) → per-decision edge ledger
Their scorecard compares each quote to the model-optimal one and separates *flow-reading* (δ term) from *inventory management* (S−δ term) — computable from observables at decision time, unlike PnL which mixes skill with luck.
- **Port:** standing diagnostic, not a model change: log predicted p_up / expected edge at entry for every trade, then bucket realized outcomes by entry-time imbalance (and other state). Separates "does the model read flow?" (calibration by imbalance decile) from "does the model manage risk?" — answers what blended OOS PnL cannot.
- **Effort:** M. **Verdict: TEST-LATER** — adopt when doing the next model comparison (v1/v2/v3-style), where it complements the PnL-decomposition idea from the 2608.19389 digest.

### F. Win-curve / fill-ratio machinery (§5 first use)
Optimal markup sits w beyond strike ⇒ constant margin s·w per won trade; fill ratio e^(−m/w) log-linear with slope −1/w estimates width from a quote archive; e^(−1) ≈ 37% optimal fill at zero strike.
- **Verdict: SKIP.** We place no quotes and observe no fill curve; nothing to estimate w from. Revisit only if an execution/slippage leg is ever built.

### G. Zero-parameter falsification of the symmetry itself (§5 second use)
Direction counts give q, win curve gives w, and δ follows with no free parameter — a clean testable prediction cross-sectionally.
- **Verdict: SKIP** for the engine; note as a cheap sanity check only if we ever get RFQ/dealer data.

## What NOT to import
- **The closed-form magnitudes as tradable constants.** δ, γ, M(q) presuppose an observable market width w and a stationary arrival imbalance q. We have neither at daily frequency; "no empirical claims" is stated outright.
- **Steady-state/ergodic framing.** The theorem holds in the stationary core (authors flag clustered arrivals and stochastic q as the companion paper's extension). Our markets are regime-switching — treat imbalance as a slowly-varying *feature*, never as a constant.
- **A market-making/quote-skewing leg.** This is dealer-side OTC pricing with adverse-selection concession ε per fill; our pipeline takes positions at marks with no quoting role. Nothing here suggests building one.
- **The exponential win curve globally.** Remark 2 weakens it to local constancy at operating strikes — irrelevant to us, but a reminder that any analogous local-calibration assumption we make should be checked at the operating point, not globally.
